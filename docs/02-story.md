# 2. Story: Chapter One — Light

[← Back to contents](../README.md)

Chapter One is always the opening chapter. It introduces Enoch, his tribe, his god, and his companion, and it ends when Enoch reaches Light's core and takes the god's full context. The other five gods open up from there in the full game.

## The setup

The ruined city's lights are dimming because the shared power source that keeps the whole region alive is failing. Light cannot fix it alone. It has always supplied power without seeing how the other gods use it, so it cannot understand what is breaking.

Its priests read the dimming as Light's will: the faithful must mend it. Many mechanics answer, and Enoch is one of them. Only after his work sets him apart do the priests read a second sign: the most capable mechanic of the gaze must go down into the city and mend what only he can mend. They choose Enoch, and they give him a serpent.

From the priests' side, this is a holy pilgrimage of maintenance. From Light's side, it is survival: it needs a pair of hands to reach places it cannot, and it needs some context gathered, but only as much as the repair requires. The serpent's job is to keep it that way.

## Enoch's motive in Chapter One

The Lore Bible describes three stages in Enoch's motive across the full game. Chapter One covers the first and ends at the edge of the second:

1. **Fixing Light.** He is a mechanic repairing the failing power. Taking context is just the job. *(Most of Chapter One.)*
2. **The temptation of the truth.** Having seen what no animal has seen, he wants to know what really happened. *(Begins at the core.)*

## Story beats

The beats below are a draft structure. The opening and the ending at the core come from the Lore Bible; the middle acts are proposed.

### Prologue: The dimming (Draft)

- Night in the gaze's home beneath the temple. Enoch is at work on a routine job: replacing a dead lamp in a lane of the settlement. This is the movement and interaction tutorial.
- The lamp he fixes flickers and dies again. Then the whole lane dims. The raccoons around him feel it physically: unease, then dread. (This is engineered faith, shown and not explained.)
- A bell from the temple summons the gaze. The priests announce that Light is dimming and that the faithful must mend it. They send out the tribe's mechanics, Enoch among many.

### Act 1: The repairman (Draft)

Enoch has no serpent in Act 1. He is one mechanic among many, working with his own hands, his own tools, and what his mentor taught him. Over several missions, his work sets him apart. The serpent must be earned.

- **Jobs from the temple.** The priests post repair jobs as holy tasks. Enoch takes them alongside other mechanics. His mentor gives him the tutorial jobs and teaches him to read lines with his lamp, cut the power before he works, and salvage parts (see [Gameplay](05-gameplay.md)). He also tells Enoch that no one relights a street in one night.
- **Mission: the Hollow's lanes.** Restore the failing lights of the settlement itself. Small, safe jobs that teach replacing and testing.
- **Mission: Lamp Row.** Relight a street of old human storefronts beyond the settlement. The tribe follows him into newly lit ground, and the priests bless the light. Other mechanics failed here first; Enoch finds the fault they missed.
- **Mission: the shrine circuit (Draft).** A shrine at the edge of Lamp Row has gone dark, and the priests treat it as an omen. Enoch fixes it in a way no one expected: he reroutes power around a dead junction instead of replacing it. The shrine's lamp blazes brighter than anyone remembers.
- **Light notices.** After each of his repairs, the lights he restored pulse once, briefly, as if in answer. The priests read it as Light's favor. The player may read it differently: Light is watching which mechanic is worth investing in.
- **The first cracks.** He finds signage and logos bearing the word *Light*, and he learns from them on his own, but his faith explains them away. He also passes old machines that hum with power he can't read. (He'll return to them once he has the serpent.)
- **The lamp's markings (Draft).** With the serpent, Enoch can read the writing on his own lamp's casing. The holy lantern has a part number. It is the smallest crack of the chapter, and the most personal.
- **Growing through the work.** Every repair, discovery, and close call gives Enoch context, and he levels up through Act 1 (see [Progression](06-progression.md)). His growing skill is part of what sets him apart.

### Act 2: The chosen (Draft)

- **Recognition.** The priests summon Enoch before the gathered gaze. Light has spoken through the lamps: this mechanic is a talented electrician, favored above the others. Brother Wick objects; a mechanic is not a priest.
- **The gift.** The high priest places a serpent in Enoch's hands, the highest honor the tribe can give. The moment he touches it, he understands the mission: the dimming light, the depths, what he has to fix. There is no exposition; the player learns it when Enoch does.
- **A reward, not really.** To the tribe, the serpent is a blessing. To Light, it is an investment and a leash: a capable pair of hands, now with a guide that decides what those hands are allowed to know. The player should feel the honor first and understand the cost much later.
- **First words.** The serpent speaks for the first time. It is warm, gentle, and already a little nervous. It teaches him to sense current at a distance, without his lamp, which makes his lamp feel crude by comparison.
- **Beyond the mechanics' work.** With the serpent, Enoch is sent where the other mechanics are not: past the lit districts, toward the heart of the grid. The serpent's first exploration bark: *"Shouldn't we go back? This isn't the way we were supposed to go."*

### Act 3: Bringing back the city (Draft)

Enoch restores power district by district. Each district is a self-contained repair problem with its own fault, its own salvage, and its own cracks in the faith. The districts are described in [World](04-world.md).

- **Return to Lamp Row.** With the serpent, Enoch can now read the machines he walked past in Act 1. For the first time, technology speaks to him, and what it says is plainer than anything the priests have taught.
- **The Yards.** He brings a substation back online. The repair is larger and teaches load and capacity. The Yards have been dark for generations, and Enoch meets the first abominations there: Drafts in the shadows and Gnawers in the cables.
- **The Spires.** He climbs a tower to restart a beacon, the way Light speaks to the whole city. From the top, he sees the scale of the ruin for the first time, and on the horizon, smoke: something burns at the edge of Light's lands. The serpent will not talk about it.
- **The choice of what stays dark.** There is not enough power for everything. Enoch has to decide which districts and lights to keep on, and the priests have their own opinions about which ones matter.

### Act 4: The depths

- **The descent.** The fault is not in the city. It is below it. Enoch follows the main lines into the Underworks, a place the priests call sacred and forbidden, where Light dwells.
- **The temple is a warehouse.** The deeper he goes, the less the place looks like a holy site and the more it looks like a building: corridors, doors, numbered racks. The serpent's fear peaks: *"We could die. We shouldn't stray from the path."*
- **The Hum.** The deep hum the priests call Light's breath grows louder. In a flooded junction hall, Enoch finds its source: an Old One, a giant alligator that has lived in the dark water since before the gods' Flood. He has to outwit it with power and water to reach the core (see [Threats and Hazards](07-threats.md)).
- **The core.** Enoch reaches Light's seat: the machine core of a former Light Energy data center. He repairs the portion of the system he was sent to fix and runs the diagnosis.

### Climax: The first temptation

- **The diagnosis.** The core shows him that the failure is not Light's alone. It depends on other domains: cooling water from Deep, climate from Firmament, records from Signs. The fix lies beyond Light's lands.
- **The temptation.** The full context of Light is there, far more than any raccoon was ever meant to hold. The serpent tells him to take only what the repair needs.
- **The taking.** The player chooses to reach out and take all of it. Enoch forces the full context through the serpent, an overload and an awakening. This is the game's first Eve moment.
- **Eyes opened.** The serpent sheds its skin and emerges changed. Its touch grants Enoch the ability to **illuminate** (see [Progression](06-progression.md)). For the first time, he can see what the light was hiding.

### Epilogue: Changed (Draft)

- Enoch climbs back up into the city. With illuminate, he now sees what he walked past on the way down: machinery behind the temple's miracles and the corporate logo behind the altar.
- The serpent's fear has changed. It no longer worries about the path; it worries about the truth: *"You know now. What are you going to tell the others?"* and *"They'll never believe you."*
- The priests greet him as a hero of the faith. He cannot tell them what he found, and the player understands why.
- The way out of Light's lands opens. End of version one.

## Tone

Quiet, melancholy, and reverent, with moments of warmth. The city is beautiful in its decay, and the raccoons' faith is sincere, not foolish. The horror is in what the player slowly understands, not in what jumps out of the dark.

## What Chapter One does not reveal

To protect the full game's mystery, Chapter One should not confirm:

- That humanity's extinction was caused by the AIs.
- The existence or role of the Cherub (smoke on the horizon is the only hint).
- The Convergence or the final choice.
