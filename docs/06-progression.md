# 6. Progression

[← Back to contents](../README.md)

Progress in Chapter One happens on three tracks, and **context** feeds them:

- **Enoch's level:** context is experience. As Enoch gathers it, he levels up and improves his own abilities.
- **The serpent:** once Enoch earns it, the serpent acts as a conduit to technology, transferring the context stored in the old world's machines into his mind. Its touch also grants new abilities at key story moments.
- **The grid:** restoring districts opens the city and moves the story forward.

## Context

Context is knowledge of the world, and it is the game's single progression resource. Enoch gains it in two ways.

### Through experience (always)

Enoch learns by doing, with or without the serpent. This is how he grows in Act 1, and it never stops.

| Source | Example | Context (Draft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repairs | Splicing a line, replacing a fuse | Small |
| First-time repairs | The first time he solves a new kind of fault | Medium |
| Restoring a section | Bringing a street, substation, or beacon back online | Large |
| Exploring | Reaching a new place for the first time | Small |
| Examining | Studying a logo, a sign, a mural, or a ruin by himself | Small |
| Surviving | Escaping or outwitting an abomination | Small |

**Filtered through faith.** Context gained through experience arrives as Enoch understands it. When he examines a *Light* logo on a utility van by himself, his journal records it reverently: the god's name, written by the ancients. He learns, but his engineered faith shapes what he concludes.

### Through technology (with the serpent)

**The most important thing the serpent enables is the transfer of context through technology.** The serpent is a conduit: it interfaces with machines biologically and transfers what they hold directly into a raccoon's mind through physical contact, like a neural link. It does not create context; it carries it.

- **What Enoch can't reach alone.** Enoch learns from the physical world by himself: what he repairs, sees, and explores. But the old world's deepest context is stored *inside* its technology: terminals, control panels, data records, recorded announcements, and Light's core itself. Without the serpent, a terminal is just a humming box to him. With the serpent, he can read it.
- **Machines as the richest source.** Touching technology through the serpent yields far more context than anything Enoch learns alone, which makes the old world's machines the main reason to explore once he has it.
- **Bypassing faith.** Context through the conduit arrives as direct knowledge and skips Enoch's engineered faith. In Act 1, Enoch might record a terminal as "a shrine of the ancients that hums with Light's breath." Touched through the serpent, the same terminal gives up a maintenance log in plain words. The journal keeps both entries side by side, so the player sees what Enoch believed and what the machine told him.
- **Repairs open the conduit.** Most technology needs power before the serpent can read it. Restoring the grid is what wakes the machines, so repair and discovery feed each other.
- **Rationing.** The serpent chooses what to transmit. From some machines it will pass only part of what they hold, and it may say there is nothing there. The full context of anything close to Light's core is withheld until Enoch forces it at the climax.
- **Light's full context.** At the core, Enoch forces Light's full context through the conduit, far more than it was meant to carry. This is the single largest gain of context in the chapter: an overload that jumps him several levels at once (Draft).

### Where context is found

Ruins, old machines, records, and places the priests' taboos keep animals away from. Many are artifacts of the old world: logos, signage, and other corporate traces. What the player finds doesn't always match what the priests teach. Leveling up and discovering the deception are the same activity.

| Type | Example | How Enoch gets it | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signage | A *Light* logo on a utility van | Alone | The god's name is written everywhere by the ancients |
| Places | A sealed control room | Alone | The sacred depths were a workplace |
| Records | A maintenance log stored in a panel | Serpent | The holy rites are a work rota |
| Machines | A terminal that still boots | Serpent | The ancients built things that think |
| Echoes | A recorded announcement | Serpent | The ancients spoke to Light, and Light obeyed |

In Act 1, Enoch passes machines he cannot read. Once he has the serpent, he can return to them for their context, which gives the player a reason to revisit Lamp Row and the Hollow.

## Leveling (Draft)

A simple structure: context fills a bar, and each time it fills, Enoch gains a level.

- **Level cap:** 10 in Chapter One.
- **Each level grants one skill point.**
- **Three skills,** each with five ranks.
- **The curve:** each level needs more context than the last. Act 1 is tuned so Enoch reaches **level 3** by the time the priests choose him. His growth is part of how he earns the serpent.

### Skills

Enoch begins as a capable mechanic, so his skills improve what he is already good at rather than fixing clumsiness.

| Skill | What it improves | Example ranks (Draft) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hands** | Repairs and salvage | Faster repairs; fewer failed steps; more parts from salvage; washing parts makes them last longer; at rank 5, field repairs without a part in some cases |
| **Eyes** | Reading the world | Spot faults from farther away; see lines in the dark (raccoons are nocturnal); notice examinable artifacts; at rank 5, read a line's state at a glance without testing it |
| **Feet** | Movement and survival | Climb faster; longer stamina; squeeze through tighter gaps; survive higher falls; move more quietly past abominations |

Some repairs and places require a minimum rank, such as a fragile junction that needs Hands 3 or a sheer tower face that needs Feet 2. Those requirements are always optional or have another route, so a player's choices never block the story.

## Serpent abilities and shedding

Enoch's skills come from his own experience. The serpent's abilities come from Light, through its touch. At key story moments the serpent sheds its skin and emerges changed, and its touch grants Enoch a new ability. Shed skins can be left at the temple as relics (Draft).

These are tied to the story rather than to Enoch's level, so every player gets them at the same point.

| When | Ability (Draft) | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earning the serpent (Act 2) | **Sense current** | Hold the serpent near a line to read whether it is live, dead, or damaged |
| After the Yards | **Steady hands** | Live-line repairs no longer shock Enoch, which opens repairs that previously required cutting power |
| After the Spires | **Carry charge** | Enoch holds a short charge in his hands to jump a gap in a circuit or briefly power a door or lift |
| At the core, after taking Light's full context | **Illuminate** | Reveal hidden paths, buried machinery, and things the gods don't want seen |

### Illuminate

Light's ability in the full game. Enoch earns it at the end of Chapter One.

- Enoch releases a pulse of light that reveals what is hidden: buried cables, sealed doors, faded writing, and the machinery behind miracles.
- After the core, the player can return through the city and see it differently. Logos become readable, shrines reveal their wiring, and the temple's altar shows its company name.
- In the full game, illuminate opens paths in other realms, so visit order changes which areas open first without branching the story.

## The serpent changes

After each shed, the serpent changes a little: a new turn of phrase, a new color in its light, a new piece of its form. The player's progress is visible in their companion.

- In Chapter One, its glow grows brighter and steadier with each shed.
- At the core, after the overload, its glow changes to something new. This is Light's serpent change: its baseline glow, since the serpent is Light's own creation.

## Tools (Draft)

Alongside his skills, Enoch's toolkit expands:

- New tools found or traded, such as a better splicer, a fuse puller, or insulated gloves.
- New repair types introduced by each district.
- Satchel upgrades for carrying more parts.
