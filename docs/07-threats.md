# 7. Threats and Hazards

[← Back to contents](../README.md)

Enoch is a mechanic, not a warrior. Danger in Chapter One is something to avoid, outwit, or survive. There is no weapon combat in version one. The one boss battle is fought with the environment and the grid, not with weapons (Draft).

## The abominations

The abominations are **the mistakes of the Image laboratory.** Image is the god of genetics, and it breeds the bodies for every god's workforce, including the raccoons. Every tribe was preceded by failed attempts, and those failures survive as abominations: less intelligent, dangerous creatures that roam the ruins, the facilities, and the wastes. The dogs keep most of them contained, but the dark corners of Light's city still hold some.

Chapter One never explains where they came from. The serpent calls them "the unblessed" and changes the subject (Draft). The truth belongs to the Image chapter of the full game.

### Abomination types (Draft)

| Name (Draft) | What it is | Behavior | Counterplay |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Drafts** | Failed early attempts at the raccoons: almost raccoons, with wrong proportions, unfinished hands, and no speech | Live in the dark districts and scavenge. Their eyes were never finished, so bright light hurts them. They mimic raccoon calls. | Light drives them back. Restored lamps and Enoch's lantern keep them at a distance. |
| **Gnawers** | Rodents bred oversized for labor, too big and too hungry | Hunt in packs by sound. Chew through cables, causing many of the faults Enoch has to repair. | Move quietly (Feet skill), use noise from machines as a distraction, and seal the gaps they come through |

**The Drafts are the chapter's quiet horror.** Enoch is facing his own kind's first drafts without knowing it. The player may notice the masks before he does.

### The light and the dark

- **The dark belongs to the abominations.** Unlit districts are where they live and hunt.
- **Light reclaims ground.** Every repair pushes the Drafts back and makes a district safer for the tribe.
- **But there isn't enough light for everywhere.** Because of the grid's limited capacity, choosing which circuits stay on is also choosing which streets stay dangerous. Restraint has a cost, and the tribe feels it.
- **The Gnawers keep the work going.** Their chewing causes new faults in districts Enoch has already restored, so the city never stays fixed for long.

## Boss: The Hum (Draft)

**An Old One.** A giant alligator, ancient and scarred, living in the flooded depths of the Underworks. It is one of Image's earliest experiments, from before the gods' Flood that purged their first failures. It survived the purge in the dark water, and it has been growing ever since.

- **Why it's called the Hum.** Alligators bellow with a sound so low it is felt more than heard, and it makes the surface of water dance. For generations, the raccoons have felt a deep hum rising from below the city, and the priests teach that it is Light breathing in the depths. It is the beast. This is one of the cracks Enoch finds on the way down.
- **Where.** A flooded junction hall in the Underworks, between Enoch and the core. The main power lines run through the hall, and the water is deep.
- **How it hunts.** It is slow on land and terrifyingly fast in water. It hunts by vibration, so footsteps, machines, and splashing all draw it.

### The battle

Enoch cannot fight the Hum with weapons. He fights it with what he knows: power, water, and machines.

1. **Hunted.** The hall is dark and half-flooded. Enoch must restore the hall's lights and pumps while staying out of the water. Its bellow warns him when it's close: the water starts to dance.
2. **Lure.** Restarted machines make noise, and the Hum hunts noise. Enoch uses machines as bait, and carry charge (from the Spires) to power doors and pumps for a moment, to move it where he wants it. Pumps raise and lower the water, which changes where it can reach.
3. **The current.** Enoch drives it into a flooded channel where the main line runs, then closes the breaker. The water-and-power hazard he has been avoiding all chapter becomes his weapon.

**The ending of the fight is open.** Whether Enoch kills the Hum or drives it off and seals it in the deep is a decision still to make (see [Open Questions](open-questions.md)). Either way, it should not feel triumphant. It is an old animal that was made, abandoned, and survived.

**Alternative (Draft):** a giant boar in the Yards, charging through fences and transformers. It could be a mid-chapter boss or a replacement for the Hum.

## Environmental hazards

| Hazard | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Live lines | Repairing without cutting the power risks a shock that knocks Enoch back. Two shocks in a night kill him, and one kills on a high-power line; death undoes that night's repairs. Steady hands (after the Yards) makes live repairs safe. See [Gameplay](05-gameplay.md#live-repairs-and-shocks). |
| Surges | Switching in the wrong order sends a surge down the line, blowing fuses and endpoints. |
| Falls | The city is vertical, and the Spires especially so. |
| Collapse | Ruined floors and passages give way. |
| Water and power | Flooded basements near live lines are lethal until the power is cut. |
| Dawn (Draft) | Raccoons are nocturnal. Each night ends at dawn; see Win and loss below. |

## Win and loss (Draft)

- **Win:** every fault on the job is repaired, the breaker is thrown, and the district relights. In the prototype, that means all of Lamp Row is lit and the Relight plays. On capacity jobs (from the Yards), the job is won once every fault is repaired and the breaker is thrown; the player then chooses with switches what gets power, and the Relight plays on that (see [Gameplay](05-gameplay.md#capacity-and-restraint)).
- **Jobs take several nights.** A job is not meant to be finished on the first visit; Enoch's mentor tells him so with his first job (see [Gameplay](05-gameplay.md#core-loop-draft)).
- **Loss: dawn.** A night lasts **15 minutes at most** *(Decided)*, and the sky shows it: the moon crosses the sky, then the horizon pales. If dawn breaks before the job is done, the night is lost: the player watches the sun come up over a street that is only partly lit while Enoch retreats underground. *(Decided.)* **The work stays done.** Every repair holds, and the next night starts in the Hollow with only the remaining faults left. *(Decided.)*
- **Loss: death by shock.** A failed live repair shocks Enoch. A second shock in one night kills him, and one shock on a high-power line kills him outright. The night is lost, and **every repair made that night is undone**, with the parts used on them gone and their faults back with their ribbons tied. Repairs from earlier nights stay. Enoch keeps his satchel and his favor, and the next night starts in the Hollow. *(Decided.)* This makes live repairs a high-risk, high-reward choice.
- **The Relight can't be interrupted.** Once the last breaker throw starts the Relight, the job is won, even if dawn is close.
- **The night runs on game time.** The night clock and the sky advance only while the game is running. Each frame's step is capped, so returning to a hidden tab never jumps the clock forward. The game pauses when the player presses **Esc**, when the tab is hidden, and when the window loses focus. A live repair in progress when the game pauses is cancelled, like letting go early: the part is not used and there is no shock. Because the pass mark is counted in nights, pausing can't skew it. *(Draft.)*
- **What always carries over:** the prayer ribbons stay tied, so the player keeps what they learned even after a death. A repair in progress when a night is lost is cancelled, and its part stays in the satchel.
- **Setbacks, not losses:** being caught by an abomination sends Enoch back to the last lit safe point, such as a shrine or a repaired fuse box, and costs him time before dawn. Completed repairs stay completed.
- The tone stays melancholy, not punishing: no harsh game over screen, just the sun rising on a street that is still dark.

## Foreshadowing the full game

- **Smoke on the horizon.** Fire is forbidden to every tribe but the dogs. From the Spires, Enoch sees smoke at the border, and the serpent avoids the subject.
- **Scorched ground.** At the very edge of the city, a burned ruin marks a place where something was destroyed. The serpent calls it a warning.
- **An owl (Draft).** Once, an owl watches Enoch from a high perch and is gone when he looks again.
